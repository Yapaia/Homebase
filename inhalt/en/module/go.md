## Why another navigation app?

Phone navigation stops where the signal stops – usually right at the mountain pass. Truck sat-navs know your weight, but they cost a lot and age badly. Yapaia Go is the third way: the routing technology logistics companies use, fed with the real dimensions of **your** vehicle, on a computer you probably already have on board.

## What Go does for you

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Routes for your size** | A 3.20 m tall camper is not sent under a 3 m bridge. Height, width, length and weight go into every route. |
| **Truly offline** | Not “works offline once cached”. Map, routing and address search live on your disk. There is no online mode to fall back to. |
| **Turn-by-turn guidance** | Spoken instructions, automatic rerouting, arrival time, speed limit and a warning when you are too fast. |
| **Any position source** | A USB GPS receiver, your browser’s GPS, or the Home Assistant app on your phone. |
| **Made for driving** | Large buttons, controls for left- and right-hand drive, settings lock above 10 km/h. The screen stays awake. |
| **Connected to Home Assistant** | Arrival, distance remaining, next instruction and location become entities – and “destination reached” a trigger for your automations. |

## Simply in the browser

Go runs on the mini-PC and opens in any browser: the tablet on the dashboard, your phone, the iPad on the passenger seat. No app, no store, no forced updates.

## Maps, ready-baked

You can build maps directly in Go – or load finished packages from the **Yapaia map server**: map, address search and routing in one go, verified by checksum. The manual explains how under [Maps and map server](/en/docs/handbuch/karten/).

## To be honest

- Go is **not a phone app**. It runs on a computer in your vehicle and is used in the browser.
- There is **no live traffic**. That is the price of being truly offline.
- Maps are as good as **OpenStreetMap**. Height and weight limits are well mapped on main roads and patchy on small ones. Go tells you about this.
